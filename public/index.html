How a lens works
  1. A field — one number the app measures at every pixel, like how fast that part of the image is moving.
  2. A ramp — the colours that number maps to, low to high.
  3. Optionally a second field driving the brightness, so one picture can carry two measurements at once.

A lens recolours a measurement. It cannot create one, and where nothing was measured it paints nothing rather than guessing.

Record checking…

avc1.42E01E is H.264 Level 3.0, whose frame ceiling is 1620 macroblocks. A 548×732 frame is 1610 — ten short of the limit — and every larger setting here is over it (1033×775 is 3185, even 720p is 3600). If an encoder honours the level it is handed, that one string would cap recordings at almost exactly the size that keeps appearing. Chromium refuses every codec-parameterised string outright and takes only plain MP4; what Safari does is not known from here. Record the same scene on each setting and compare the encoded size in the log below — that is the number that settles it.

How recording works, and what its frame rate means

Records what is on screen — the filter as well as the camera — in clips of at most 30 seconds. Each clip is a finished file the moment it is cut, so a call or a lock screen costs the clip in hand and nothing already recorded.

Several filters compute at a small analysis frame for speed, and the recording is scaled up from it to the size you actually see. That is the picture on screen, not extra detail: Live detail above is what makes the filter itself render larger, and it costs frame rate. The camera on its own records at its own full resolution with no scaling.

Recording detail lifts the preview's ceiling. A filter normally renders no larger than this screen's own logical pixel count — the cap that stopped the preview lagging — which is right for something you are watching live and wrong for a file you will watch full screen later. Raising it applies while recording only, and it costs frame rate in proportion: roughly 0.4 megapixels a frame at Match, 0.8 at Higher, and up to about 2 at Full on this phone. An unfiltered recording ignores all of this and uses the camera's own resolution.

Three rates, and they are three different things. The camera delivers frames at one rate, the vision pipeline analyses at another, and the recording gets a frame every time a filtered picture is actually redrawn. On a heavy filter the last one is the slowest, and it is the one the file contains — measured, not requested, and shown while recording. A recorder cannot invent frames the pipeline never drew, so if the number is low the levers are Live detail and the vision rate in Settings, not the recorder.

What a GIF costs

No browser can encode a GIF, so this app writes the file itself. The format holds 256 colours and compresses each frame on its own, so a few seconds of camera can outweigh a thirty-second video — keep them short and small. What it buys is that a GIF plays inline anywhere, in any message, with no player and no codec question.

Only what is inside the box is measured — the centre of the sensor at full resolution, not the whole frame. Fill it with texture: gravel, foliage, brickwork, fabric weave. A blank wall or a flat sky inside the box leaves nothing to measure movement against, however detailed the rest of the view is.

Holding still beats moving deliberately, which is the opposite of what it sounds like. Detail is recovered from where the camera sits within a pixel, so a shift of seven pixels and a shift of a fifth of a pixel count the same — travelling further adds nothing and blurs each frame while it does it. Ordinary tremor is already the right size.

The field of view is measured from the burst, not looked up — no browser reports it. Turning the phone a little while capturing is what makes it measurable. Type a value only to override.

Sensor Startup & Steadiness

A web page cannot hold or extend a browser permission — that grant belongs to Safari and to iOS, and nothing here changes how long it lasts. What these remember is that you wanted a sensor running, so it starts on its own when the browser already says yes. Where a tap is still required, one tap anywhere starts everything that is waiting.

Auto-start is off for every sensor.

Sensors are released when the app is backgrounded and restored when it returns, so nothing left running keeps the GPS or the camera awake behind your back.

Handshake calibration

Hold the phone the way you actually mean to hold it and calibrate for ten seconds. That measures how still this device is in your hand, and everything below the measured floor is then treated as tremor rather than movement. It is a gate, not stabilisation — it does not remove camera movement from the picture, it stops the app reporting your own hand as an event.
